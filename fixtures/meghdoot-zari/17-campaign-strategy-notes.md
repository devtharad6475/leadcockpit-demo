# Campaign and Outreach Strategy

## Season

| Period | Activity |
|---|---|
| August – November | Peak. Festival and wedding season. Wholesalers stock in July–August, manufacturers buy September–October. |
| February – April | Second peak. Spring wedding season. |
| May – July | Lean. Job workers still buy small quantities; wholesalers mostly do not. |

Outreach should be heaviest in June–July (ahead of the first peak) and January (ahead of the
second). Reaching a wholesaler in October, when their buying is already done, wastes the contact.

## Channel

WhatsApp is primary. This trade runs on WhatsApp — buyers, brokers and job workers all live
in it, and a message gets read where an email does not.

Email is secondary, used for quotations, invoices and anything that needs a paper trail.

We do not cold call.

## What we lead with

Our positioning is **shade consistency, range, and on-time dispatch**. Not lowest price.

The strongest opening for a new contact is the shade card offer. It is free, it is genuinely useful,
and a buyer who accepts it has told us they are real.

The second strongest is a shade-matching problem. A buyer who has been let down on
consistency by their current supplier is our most likely conversion, and they are easiest to find
mid-season.

## What we never do in outreach

- No messages without consent
- No messages outside working hours
- No messages to anyone who has opted out
- No price in an outreach message
- No claims about competitors
- No more than one message a day to the same contact
- No more than three follow-ups before marking the lead cold

## Segment-specific angles

| Segment | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Saree wholesalers | Batch-to-batch consistency across a season |
| Garment / lehenga manufacturers | On-time dispatch and published delivery windows |
| Embroidery job workers | Stock availability and small repeat quantities |
| Boutiques / designers | 60+ shade range and real zari quality |
| Trim distributors | Full-range supply from one source |
