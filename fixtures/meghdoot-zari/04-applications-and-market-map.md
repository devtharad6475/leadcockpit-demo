# Applications and Market Map

## Where our products end up

**Sarees.** The single largest application. Zari thread goes into the pallu and body work,
borders go onto the edge. Surat and Varanasi wholesalers drive this volume.

**Lehengas and bridal wear.** Heavy border work and real zari. Lower volume, higher value per
order, concentrated in Delhi, Jaipur and Mumbai.

**Dress material and suits.** Imitation and metallic zari in fine counts. High repeat volume,
price-sensitive.

**Blouse and panel embroidery.** Job workers buy thread, sequins and cutdana together for
piece-rate embroidery work.

**Home furnishing.** Cushion covers, curtain borders, table runners. A small but growing line,
mostly through trim distributors.

**Festive and religious textiles.** Temple cloth, chunri, festival wear. Strongly seasonal.

## Which product goes to which buyer

| Buyer type | Buys most | Buys sometimes | Cares most about |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saree wholesaler | Imitation zari, saree borders | Real zari, sequins | Rate and shade consistency across a season |
| Garment / lehenga manufacturer | Real zari, lehenga borders, laces | Cutdana | On-time dispatch and shade matching |
| Embroidery job worker | Imitation and metallic zari, sequins, cutdana | Narrow laces | Availability from stock, small repeat quantities |
| Boutique / designer | Real zari, wide laces, bridal borders | Custom shades | Shade range and being able to order below wholesale volume |
| Trim distributor | Full range in bulk | Custom job work | Credit terms and consistent supply |

## What creates demand

Buying intent in this trade is driven by calendar, not by advertising.

- **August to November** — festival and wedding season. Our peak. Wholesalers stock in July
  and August for it; manufacturers buy through September and October.
- **February to April** — spring wedding season. The second peak.
- **May to July** — lean. Job workers still buy small quantities; wholesalers mostly do not.
- **New design launches** — when a wholesaler launches a design, they buy thread and matching
  border together and repeat through the season if it sells.
- **Shade matching failures elsewhere** — a buyer whose previous supplier delivered an
  off-shade batch will switch mid-season. These convert fast and are worth prioritising.

## Signals that a lead is real

A serious buyer in this trade names their business, names their city, names a count or a design
number, and gives a quantity in kilograms or metres. A buyer who does none of these is browsing.

The strongest single signal is a buyer asking for a rate on a *specific* count — for example
"what is the rate on 200/2 imitation" rather than "what is your rate". Specificity means they
already know what they need.
