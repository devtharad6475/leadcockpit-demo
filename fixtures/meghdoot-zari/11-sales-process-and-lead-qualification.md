# Sales Process and Lead Qualification

## The stages

1. **Enquiry** — a buyer makes contact
2. **Qualify** — collect business name, city, product interest, approximate quantity
3. **Sample / catalogue** — shade card, cut samples, catalogue PDF
4. **Quotation** — issued by Priya, in writing, valid 7 days
5. **Negotiation** — rate, quantity, terms. Owner approval for anything beyond policy.
6. **Order confirmed** — advance payment received
7. **Production and dispatch**
8. **Follow-up** — repeat order, feedback, next season

Note that stage 4 is the first stage a human must do. Stages 1 to 3 can be handled without
Priya's involvement; stage 4 cannot.

## What the first response must collect

Four things, every time:

- business name
- city
- which product they are interested in
- approximate quantity

Ask for all four in one message rather than dragging it across five exchanges. Buyers in this
trade are busy and answer a single clear question faster than a conversation.

## Marketing qualified lead

A lead is marketing qualified when it has:

- a B2B business name, **and**
- a city, **and**
- a stated product interest

That is the bar. It is deliberately low, because in this trade a named business in a known city is
worth a follow-up even without a quantity.

## Sales qualified lead

A lead is sales qualified when it is marketing qualified **and**:

- quantity is at or above our minimum order quantity, **and**
- the timeline is within 30 days, **and**
- we are speaking to someone who can decide

## Reject

- Retail buyers and individuals wanting a few pieces
- Students and hobbyists
- Anyone wanting less than the minimum who is not a business
- Anyone outside India

Rejection is polite and brief. Offer the shade card if they are a business that may grow.

## Hot lead signals

Any one of these moves a lead to the top of the list:

- asks for a rate on a **specific count**, e.g. "rate for 200/2 imitation"
- asks for sample dispatch
- mentions a festival or wedding-season deadline
- quantity of 25 kg or more
- mentions a shade-matching problem with their current supplier

## Cold signals

- "just checking rates"
- no business name after being asked
- no quantity and no timeline
- has asked the same question three times across months without ordering

## Follow-up cadence

Day 2, day 5, day 12. After that, mark cold and stop. Three follow-ups is enough — a fourth
makes us look desperate and this market talks.

Follow-up only during working hours, only with consent, and never more than once a day.
